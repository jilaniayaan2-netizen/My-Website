Come on in! Welcome to the Nature Zone

I've always loved nature and some of my best memories come from being out in the woods, by the river, or even in my own backyard. So, with this in mind, it only made sense to put a whole page dedicated to nature.

Some Stories

There's no better way to start out a page about nature other than some good ol' stories. So, pretend that you're around a campfire, grab a snack, and learn about my misadventures in the wilderness.

Lost in the Woods

This is probably a story that almost everybody can relate to, so I'll keep it short and sweet. A few years ago, being the pea-brained preteen I was, I went to the forest by myself to look for salamanders. 3 hours and 20 salamanders later, it was time to head back . . . and I was completely lost. The next hour was filled with google maps tweaking out, sending voice messages to my friends like a captain lost at sea, and seeing the same tree 4 different times. Luckily, after the 10th loop up the hill, I found a paved pathway which I followed all the way out of the forest and got home by nightfall.

A Muddy Hell

This story takes place from my first time going dipnetting in a new part of the river. If you don't know what dipnetting is, it's where you wade around in the water with a net to catch fish. In the part of the river where I usually dipnetted, it was pretty rocky, and that's what I expected with this part of the river. I was very wrong. 20 minutes in and I was over ankle-deep in river mud, contemplating what choices I made to get here. Using the sheer power of the indomitable human spirit, I freed myself and didn't even lose my sandals.

My Hand and the Water Serpent

You see, in my city there are 5 species of snake: The Eastern Garter Snake, The Dekay's Brownsnake, The Eastern Milksnake, The Red-bellied Snake, and lastly, the subject of this story: The Common Water Snake. I was a pretty dumb pre-teen as you could probably imagine and I tended to pick up most snakes I found: most of those snakes being Eastern Garter Snakes. So, when I went to the river and saw a snake swimming down the river, I immediately grabbed it and just as quickly had judgement delivered. Completely unrelated, I learned that Common Water Snakes have an anti-coagulant in their saliva, which prevents the blood from clotting and causes you to bleed for longer.

Sixteen Snakes, Two Days

During the early days of April 2026, I was skipping Calculus(as one does) but I didn't want to just head straight home just yet, so instead I took the oppertunity to drop by a local creek near my school. Now mind you, this creek is an incredibly urban creek. It is practically smack dab in the middle of the city, so obviously, I wasn't expecting to find much while walking it's paved trails(other than common birds like Robins, Gackles, Cardinals, etc) but while I'm walking, I hear rustling in the leaves. Ussually I would assume that it's just the wind but I glanced over regardless of that, that's where I spotted 2 Common Garter Snakes. I got them for my inaturalist but now I had a mission to find more, where there's 1 snake, there's gotta be more. That day I ended off with finding 3 snakes, including the beauty in the picture for this story. I went back the next day during my lunch and spare, that day was truely insane. The day started off strong when I went down to the bank of the creek and spotted a snake. The luck kept on going and by the end I found a total of 13 snakes, I also saw 3 snakes all grouped up together, likely recouvering from brumation. For a creek right in the city, I was beyond surprised.

A Baby Turtle named Moses?

After another incredibly succesfully day of dipnetting where I really pushed some records, catching various species I don't ussually catch such as Pumpkinseed Sunfish and even catching my PB Stonecat, I had begun heading upstream to exit the river. As I walk against the current, I notice a little blob floating downstream towards me. On a closer look, it was a baby snapping turtle! I quickly scooped him up and out of the water, I was very nervous and thought the little guy may have drowned. Thankfully, he pulled through and I placed him in the shallows so that he wouldn't be at risk of going into the main river.

INaturalist

INaturalist is a platform I found back in 2023 and ever since then, I've been absolutely hooked. INaturalist in a nutshell is like irl pokemon, you take pictures of animals, plants, fungi, etc and then it's stored for you so that you can see how many species and how many individuals you've observed. Now, obviously, it's a great tool for collecting data about various species for researchers but the main reason I use it is because of the leaderboard. For each species there's a leaderboard to see who has observed it the most, there's a global leaderboard and there's also leaderboards for cities, countries, etc. With all this said, all the animals on the right are the ones I'm ranked number one for in my city.

Finding a very Special Turtle

If you look to the right, you'll see one species stands out from the others, that being the only turtle on there, The Blanding's Turtle. The reason why it gets a whole section dedicated to it is because . . . drumroll please . . . in the entirity of my city, I am the ONLY one to have an observaton of the Blanding's Turtle. So ya, I think that's pretty neat.

Wringing it all out

INaturalist in it's nature isn't a competetive app, its an app meant for people to help with science. While I admire that about INaturalist and enjoy it for that reason, they also added leaderboards for species in specific areas and even globally. Seeing the leaderboard I quickly began climbing up the leaderboard in my city and currently I am at 6th place for animals overall. However, for fish, I had quickly and without much pushback, climbed up to the number 1 position with 260 observations. This summer however, I had my title contested and it felt like having a sweet dessert after a great meal. Now first, my rival, who was also the person I had overtaken in the first place, had slowly gain on me but I furthered my lead at the same time. Then one day, he suddenly surged past me, the score being 390 to 369. We then began passing the 1st place between each other, sometimes I would be number 1 but only for a few days or a week and other times, he'd sit on the throne. Now, I thought that it was one sided for the most part. Until one day, where I had surged past him by making 96 fish observations in a single day, putting the score at 612 to 550. Then the very next day, he made 74 observations in a single day just to beat me by 10. After this, I had to go on a trip to Africa so he held onto that first place and during the time of my absence increased his lead, bringing it up to 696. When I came back, I immeditly got to work and soon I had finally reclaimed my title. Currently as of october 2026, the score is 798 to 728, with me in first. I am now very excited for next summer to defend my title once more.

How to Find Gems in Your City

Finding nature gems in your city may seem incredibly difficult but it's not as hard as you think and here, I'll provide you some tips about finding these gems. Firstly, I'd recommend downloading Inaturalist. There's a pretty high chance that people in your area have clustered around one area to observe wildlife. For example, I found out about Riverwood and Erindale through this method as I saw a ton of people make observations in these two areas. Now obviously, this may not help you find a more obsecure little gem, such as the Creditview Wetland in my case. Well, you cna also try finding the website of a conservation organization for your local watershed or ecosystem. In my case, I learned more about the Creditview Wetland through looking through the CVC(Credit Valley Conservation) website. Lastly and I'd say this is the most practical tip, just explore your city with your eyes and ears open. It doesn't particularly matter how you do this, whether you do it by foot, car, or bike but just exploring your city and then making sure to notice every small detail is guranteed to find you a gem. That's how I found some of my very favorite spots like the Creditview Wetland, Credit Meadows, and Carloyn Creek. So get outside, touch grass, and find some gems of your own!

My Trip to Costa Rica

Back in 2023, I visted Costa Rica along with my family and to say it was amazing is a gross understatement. It was so biodiverse that everywhere I looked there was a new species. In fact, Costa Rica was so biodiverse that in 2 weeks, I went from 100 species to OVER 300 SPECIES. So, making a section of the Nature section dedicated to Costa Rica was a no brainer.

Tortuguero: The Boat Ride and the Night Walk

Tortegero is a small village that borders right on the Tortuguero national park. We had to take a boat ride to get there due to how remote it was and on the way there, I spotted a species I was really hoping to spot: The American Crocodile. After a very strong start to this trip, we went to the hotel, rested, and then left by nightfall for the nightwalk. The trail was completely flooded, so we had to wear boots as the guide showed us the hundreds of different species of frogs, insects, and spiders. At one point, we even saw a turtle swimming about in the flooded trail. It's impossible to describe how amazing it was seeing new species, after new species, after new species. The species I have below are, from left to right, Black River Turtle, Helmeted Iguana(which is related to chameleaons), Brazillian Wandering Spider, and the American Crocodile.

Tortuguero: The Canoe Ride and The Day Walk

The next morning, we were out on the lagoon in a canoe. It was so calm, there was absolutely no other noise other than the water and the occasional bird. Speaking of birds, the diversity of birds on this lagoon and it's creek was extrodinary. We saw many different species of herons and other fish eating birds. Other than birds, we also saw some Spectacled Caimans. After the canoe ride, we took a trail into the forest for a day walk. Which, while not as diverse as the nightwalk, still allowed us to spot many interesting species, such as land crabs, lizards, poison dart frogs, and monkeys. The species listed below are: Northern Jacana, Little Blue Heron, Spectacled Caiman, and The Strawberry Poison Dart Frog.

La Fortuna and Tamerindo

The next two places we went to in Costa Rica was La Fortuna and then Tamerindo. We didn't see many animals in La Fortuna, there most of the species I photographed were birds I saw at the resort we stayed at. The main attraction of La Fortuna was the amazing views on the hikes. After that, we were off the sandy shores of Tamerindo. There, we stayed at a resort where we saw some very chill iguanas. Then we went to do some snorkling, where I spotted a big pufferfish just chilling out in the open. Another amazing thing about Tamerindo was how clearly you could see the stars at night on the beach.

Monte Verde

So far all of the places I went to in Costa Rica were hot and humid, like you'd expect from a rainforest. Monte Verde however, was completely different. While it was just as wet as the other places, it was cold, which was due to it being up in the clouds. Here, we stayed in a cabin in the woods, which allowed me to see some cool animals such as a hummingbird and a coati, which is a creature that sorta resembles a racoon. We also did a nightwalk here, which not as amazing as the Tortuguero nightwalk was still amazing.

Crocodile Bridge and Manuel Antonio National Park

The last two places of notes we went to were Crocodile bridge, which, just as the name suggests, is a bridge where you are able to spot tons of crocodiles from. Manuel Antonio National Park was also amazing, we spotted a bunch of sloths, monkeys, and some very unique birds. The animals below are: American Crocodiles, Central American White-faced Capuchin, Terciopelo, Common Potoo, and the Brown-throated Three-toed Sloth

My Trip to Tanzania

In the summer of 2026, I went on a safari trip to Tanzani(and Zanzibar). It was truely something that you can't experiance anywhere else in the world, being able to see some of the last of Earth's megafuana all in the same place is truely like nothing else. Obviously, I do also love the small animals such insects and what not but seeing elephants just a few feet away from you is something truely special.

Tarangire National Park

Tarangire National Park was the first stop on our list with our amazing guide, Emanuel. The first thing we did was a walking safari(with park rangers of course), during which we spotted animals such as warthogs, dik-dik, impala, waterbuck, eland, wilderbeast, and tons of zebras. We also saw some much larger animals while on foot such as a lone water buffalo, which the park rangers cautiously got us away from. We also saw an elephant from afar, we didn't get to look at it for too long until the rangers ushered us away. During the walking safari, I made sure to find as many birds as I could, which included many different species of weavers, shrikes, and hornbills. Afterwards, we went on a car safari, where we got to see some more of the animals we saw on the walking safari and some other animals like ostriches and even a pair of teen lions. We also saw a whole herd of elephants, in which, two elephants had begun a bit of arguement(not a full on fight).

Central Serengeti National Park Day 1

Day 1 in the Serengeti was likely the most eventful day of the safari, we spotted a secratary bird, Grant's and Thompson's Gazelles, a family of Cheetahs, and a pride of lions. We also were able to get really close to a herd of elephants, seeing the giants up close really made you realize just how big they are. After that we saw a black backed jackle, a serval, and a leapord. Another really cool interaction was with the cheetah as the mama cheetah jumped on the back of our jeep to get a better view of the savvannah! I didn't get to see it since I was near the front but since my brothers were at the back, they saw it.

Central Serengeti National Park Day 2

Day 2 started off with a bang, I had woke up around 5am and I saw my brother at the tent door talking to one of the camp workers. Turns out he was warning my brother to stay in the tent as a pride of lions had hunted a buffalo just a few tents down! We quickly had breakfast and our guide took us to the kill site and it was quite a large pride with 2 male lions, some lionesses, and a bunch of teenage lions. After that we saw more of the animals that we've already seen such as buffalos, hyenas, elephants, various species of antelopes. We also saw a pair of lionesses on the hunt but they were unsuccesful and gave up after 2 hours of hunting. The highlight of the day however was at night which is when I found .. A DUNG BEETLE! Which was something I was really looking forward to.

Northern Serengeti National Park

We spent a total of one night in the north and the reason for that was mostly to see the wilderbeast migration. We spent the most of the time driving the central serengerti to the north but once we got to the river, it only took about half an hour for the migration to begin and when it did it was amazing to see! But we didn't waste our time sitting around, no no no, instead we went looking for some crocs and we were able to find an absolute tank basking on the river bank! Then soon after, the migration began. First it was like a race and everybody rushed towards the river to watch, then we saw thousands of these wilderbeast cross the river. Near the end of the crossing, we saw a giant croc rush down the river towards some stragglers, fortunatly the wilderbeast got across in time. After that we spent the night in the north befre we finally left the serengeti but while on the way out, we spotted 2 leopards!

Ngorongoro National Park

The next day, we went straight to Ngorongoro National Park, to spot the last of the big 5: The Rhino. The way to Ngorongoro was absolutely stunning as you could see the crater as you drive down to the park and it's such a unique landscape. It didn't take us very long to spot a rhino, now obviously, we didn't chase after the rhino or try to get close to it on purpose since that would've gotten us fined(one of the cars in front of us got fined for it). Though during the traffic jam to continue down the road, we were able to quickly get some pictures as we drove right by it. Other than the rhino, we also spotted an african wolf, greater and lesser flamingos, and gray crowned cranes. So overall, it was a great day, with the rhino being the obvious highlight.

Zanzibar

After our amazing safari on the mainland, we went to Zanzibar, which other than having some of the best food that we've had all trip, also had some really amazing fish and animals that I've captured using my underwater camera. Other than just fish, we also saw some other animals such as monkeys, a chameleon, and a gecko. The gecko I saw was the dwarf painted gecko, I sadly did not find the zanzibar day gecko which looks identical to the GEICO gecko. Another really unique animal that we saw was the elephant shrew, which I wish we could've gotten a better look at but regardless of that, he was a cute lil fella.

The Pets(Sorta)

As a kid, I've had some pets, mainly fish, but I did also have two African Dwarf Frogs, respectfully named Lightning and Thunder. Now that was about 10 years ago, now I have over 10, beautiful koi fish. Other than that, we dont keep any animals but we do have some animals that I consider to kinda be pets. First, we have about 5 squrriels that come to our backyard who we feed. They first started coming a few years ago and have been coming ever since. The fat black squrriel you see is peanut, which you can tell it's her because of how she looks up at you for food. We also have another squrriel named Chestnut, which is the brown one you see. We also have a couple others, which I haven't gotten to take pictures of but they are some strange lil chuds. As for the cats, we have about 5 cats that come. The first of which was the gray cat you see, named Aloo. We also have an older grumpy cat named Baloo, a black cat named Brownie(along with her 2 kids), and a ginger cat. Aloo hasn't come back in a very long time, so hence its 4. We also have some occasional cats that show up such as these two white patterned cats. Lastly, the most recent arrival is a skunk. Now skunks are cute and obviously we aren't delibrately feeding him but when we leave out food for the squrriels and cats, it shows up for a bite during the night.
